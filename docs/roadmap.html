Section 2

What we are building

The new site opens with a scroll-scrubbed continuous camera flight — the visitor scrolls and a camera flies through a connected world, scene to scene, with no cuts. Scroll drives time; the camera genuinely moves. This is the technique behind Apple's scroll-through product pages, built here with the scroll-world skill already in your Downloads.

It fits HelloVoice specifically: you sell cinematic craft, so the homepage should be a piece of cinematic craft rather than a grid of thumbnails describing one. Each scene in the flight becomes one of your service areas, and the copy pins over the film as the camera settles.

The tension we have to design around, stated plainly

The audit's headline complaint was weight — 31.6 MB of GIFs, 6.3 s to load. A scroll film is also heavy: eleven clips for a six-scene chain, roughly 8 MB each. If we build it naively we replace one weight problem with a bigger one.

The difference is architecture, and it is not optional. The engine loads clips lazily as blobs, shows a still poster until the video paints, prefetches only the neighbouring clip, and falls back to stills entirely under prefers-reduced-motion. First paint stays around 400 KB; the film streams in behind it. Phase 6 exists to prove that on a throttled Riyadh mobile connection, and we do not launch until it passes.

Section 3

Seven decisions I need from you

Each of these changes what gets built or what it costs. My recommendation is on each one — but they are yours to make, and Phase 1 stalls without them.

GATE 1

AI-generated world, or your own footage?

The skill's default is AI-generated isometric diorama scenes. That looks superb and is fast. But HelloVoice's entire pitch is that you shoot real premium work for Pfizer, L'Oréal and Abbott — opening with an AI cartoon world could undercut it. The scrub engine does not care where the frames come from: your editors could cut a continuous camera move from real footage instead.

RECOMMEND · Hybrid. Real footage for the opening and closing scenes, AI dioramas for the abstract middle ones (post-production, AI, CGI). Decide before any render spend.
GATE 2

How many scenes?

Scene count drives everything: render cost is N stills + (2N−1) clips. Your site currently claims six areas of expertise, which maps cleanly to six scenes.

RECOMMEND · N = 6. Five scenes if budget is tight; seven is the practical ceiling before the scroll feels long.
GATE 3

Mobile version — yes or no?

A proper mobile version means a second camera chain rendered natively in 9:16 portrait, not a crop. It roughly doubles render cost. Without it, phones get the desktop film centre-cropped, which cuts the sides off every scene — exactly the fault the audit already flagged on your current hero.

RECOMMEND · Yes. Mobile is likely the majority of your traffic, and shipping a crop repeats the mistake we are fixing.
GATE 4

Who pays for the renders — Monid or Higgsfield?

Monid bills per clip in USD with no subscription; Higgsfield bills credits against your plan. Same underlying model. Neither is ready right now: Monid is not installed on this machine, and the Higgsfield CLI is installed but not logged in.

RECOMMEND · Monid for a one-off build (pay-per-use, nothing expires). Higgsfield if you already hold credits you want to burn.
GATE 5

Keep WordPress, or rebuild the front end?

The current stack is WordPress 6.9 with Elementor Pro, 27 stylesheets and jQuery. The scrub engine is plain JavaScript and will run inside it — but the empty-section bug and the page weight both trace back to that stack.

RECOMMEND · Rebuild the front end as a static site; keep WordPress only if a non-technical person must publish case studies weekly. This is the single biggest fork in the plan.
GATE 6

Is Arabic in scope?

Riyadh office, Gulf clients, English-only site, no hreflang. Full RTL localisation is a different-sized project from an Arabic landing page — and it affects the type system, the layout and the scroll copy from day one.

RECOMMEND · Decide now even if the answer is "later". Retrofitting RTL after launch costs more than building for it.
GATE 7

Camera personality?

Three options. Fly-through dives into each scene then pulls up and hops to the next — expressive, best for miniature worlds. Walkthrough glides forward only, never pulling back — grounded and cinematic. Locked isometric holds one fixed angle throughout — calmest, cheapest to re-roll.

RECOMMEND · Walkthrough, if Gate 1 lands on real footage — a forward-only glide reads as filmmaking. Fly-through if the world goes fully AI diorama.
Section 4

The phases

0

Stop the bleeding on the live site

Week 1 · runs in parallel with everything · ~2 days
  • Repoint the Free Consultation button at the real contact page
  • Re-encode the five GIFs as looping muted MP4 — 31.6 MB down to under 1 MB
  • Install GA4 plus Meta and LinkedIn pixels; fire a conversion event on form submit
  • Add Open Graph and Twitter Card tags with a proper share image
  • Write meta descriptions and real page titles; generate and submit a sitemap
  • Fix the 2024 copyright; verify the mobile number's digit count
Output: the current site stops losing enquiries. None of this work is wasted by the rebuild.
1

Foundations and tooling

Week 1–2 · ~3 days · blocked by Gates 1–7
  • Answer the seven gates; lock scene count, camera style and budget ceiling
  • Install the missing tooling (see Section 6) and authenticate the render backend
  • Build the brand kit: 4–6 named hex values, display name, tone — pulled from the existing logo and site, then approved
  • Run one still and one clip as a calibration probe; extrapolate real cost before committing
Output: a signed-off brief, a working render pipeline, and a cost figure based on measurement rather than estimate.
2

Content and information architecture

Week 2–3 · ~5 days
  • Map the six scenes to the six service areas; write eyebrow, headline, body and tags for each
  • Define the full sitemap: home, six service pages, case-study template, filterable portfolio, about, contact
  • Choose three flagship projects and start client clearance — this takes longest, so it starts early
  • Write one real H1 per page, stated as a proposition rather than the brand name
  • Draft alt text for every image that survives into the new site
  • Specify the new enquiry form: company, service, budget band, phone, WhatsApp opt-in
Output: approved copy deck and sitemap. Nothing renders until the words exist — re-rendering a scene because the copy changed is the most expensive mistake available.
3

Art direction and cheap previz

Week 3–4 · ~4 days
  • Lock the style preamble — one paragraph reused verbatim in every scene prompt; this is what makes the world cohesive
  • Generate the six scene stills and review them side by side as one world
  • Render the entire chain at draft quality (480p) — the whole flight for a few dollars
  • Watch the draft end to end, fix pacing and scene order, re-roll what does not work
Output: an approved low-res film. Approval gate — nothing renders at full quality until you have watched this.
4

Full render

Week 4–5 · ~5 days · mostly machine time
  • Render the final desktop chain at 1080p: six dives plus five connectors
  • Frame-lock every seam — each connector starts on the previous clip's actual last frame and ends on the next clip's actual first frame
  • Render the native 9:16 portrait chain if Gate 3 said yes
  • Encode for scrubbing: native resolution, crf 20, short GOP, audio stripped
  • Budget re-rolls — the content filter flags innocuous clips and some legs need two or three attempts
Output: the finished film, desktop and mobile, encoded and ready to wire.
5

Build the site around the film

Week 5–7 · ~10 days
  • Wire the scrub engine: sections, connectors, per-scene pacing and dwell
  • Build the pages the audit found missing — six service pages, case-study template, filterable portfolio
  • Build the new enquiry form with real qualification fields
  • Image pipeline: WebP at sensible dimensions, lazy loading below the fold
  • Accessibility pass: heading order, alt text, contrast, visible focus states, keyboard navigation
  • Rename and reorganise the media library — no more 12345_00000-1.png
Output: a complete site on a staging URL, with every audit fault closed.
6

Performance and seam QA

Week 7 · ~3 days
  • Screenshot either side of every seam — they must match in composition, not just look similar
  • Throttle CPU 4–6× on an emulated phone and scroll fast; the film must track without freezing
  • Test iOS Safari specifically — it is the browser that goes blank if the video priming regresses
  • Confirm the portrait clips actually serve on mobile and the 1080p masters only on desktop
  • Measure against the budget: first paint under 400 KB, largest contentful paint under 2.5 s on mobile
  • Verify the reduced-motion path falls back cleanly to stills
Output: a signed performance report. This phase can send us back to Phase 4 — that is what it is for.
7

Launch and measure

Week 8 · ~3 days
  • Redirect map for every changed URL; re-submit the sitemap to Search Console
  • Confirm conversion tracking fires end to end, form to inbox to analytics
  • Capture the launch baseline against the audit's numbers
  • Agree the reporting cadence and who owns it
Output: live site, tracked, with a measured before-and-after.
Section 5

What the renders cost

Render spend is the only genuinely variable cost, and it is driven by Gates 2 and 3. Figures below are the skill's documented rates for a six-scene chain — six stills plus eleven clips, doubled if mobile is in scope. Phase 1's calibration probe replaces these estimates with measured numbers before anything is committed.

ScopeClipsMonid (USD)Higgsfield (credits)
Draft previz — whole chain at 480p11≈ $3≈ 120
Desktop final — 1080p11≈ $27≈ 440–605
Mobile chain — native 9:1611≈ $27≈ 440–605
Scene stills — six images6—≈ 90
Re-roll headroom — 15%—≈ $9≈ 160
Total, desktop + mobile28+≈ $66≈ 1,250–1,500

The draft tier is the cost control. Three dollars buys the entire flight at low resolution. Watching that before committing to the full render is what stops a $66 mistake from becoming a $130 one — and it is why Phase 3 has a hard approval gate.

Design and build time is separate and depends on Gate 5. A static rebuild is roughly 25–30 working days across Phases 1–7; keeping WordPress is nearer 18–22 but inherits the current stack's limits.
