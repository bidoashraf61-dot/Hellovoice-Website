Those three tell the whole story. The site is heavy, invisible to search engines and screen readers, and unmeasured. Everything below is detail on that, plus the one bug that should be fixed before you read any further.

Fix today

The Free Consultation button goes nowhere

The orange Free Consultation button sits in the header of every page. It is the site's only primary call-to-action. Its link target is a malformed page anchor, so clicking it does nothing at all — no navigation, no scroll, no form.

<a href="#hellovoice.co.uk/contact">Free Consultation</a> → should be: href="https://hellovoice.co.uk/contact-us/"

This is a one-line change in the Elementor header template. Until it is made, every click on the site's main conversion button is lost.

Performance

The homepage is built out of GIFs

The six-service section uses animated GIFs as CSS background images. GIF has no modern compression, so each one is tens of megabytes for a few seconds of motion. The same clips as looping, muted MP4 or WebM would be roughly 2–4% of the size with better image quality.

One measured load of the homepage transferred 19.2 MB across 93 requests and finished loading in 6.3 seconds — on desktop broadband, with some GIFs already cached. On a Riyadh mobile connection the first visit is far worse, and the visitor is a marketing director who has clicked through from LinkedIn and will not wait.

The build compounds it: 27 separate stylesheets, 23 scripts, jQuery plus jQuery Migrate, and no WebP or AVIF anywhere — every one of the 155 image references is PNG, JPG or GIF.

Findings

Everything else, worst first

Critical

Whole sections render as empty space

At 1440×900 the homepage contains a 2,163 px block of blank navy and six empty 701 px cells where the case-study grid should be. A visitor scrolls through several screens of nothing. The page is 10,797 px tall — about twelve full screens — and a meaningful share of that is void.

Critical

No analytics, no pixels, no measurement

There is no GA4, no Meta pixel, no LinkedIn insight tag, no Clarity or Hotjar. You cannot answer how many people visit, which services they look at, or where enquiries come from. Every recommendation in this document is currently unfalsifiable, which is itself the problem.

Critical

Shared links preview as blank

The site has no Open Graph or Twitter Card tags. When the URL is pasted into WhatsApp, LinkedIn or Slack it renders with no image, no title card, no description. For an agency that pitches over WhatsApp in the Gulf, the link you send a prospect is a first impression, and right now it is an empty grey box.

High

Not one image has alt text

Across all four pages, 93 images carry an empty alt attribute. That is a straight WCAG 2.1 failure on 1.1.1 Non-text Content, and it throws away the descriptive text that would otherwise tell Google what your portfolio actually shows.

home 26 · about 22 · portfolio 43 · contact 2 → 0 with non-empty alt
High

Three of four pages have no H1

About, Portfolio and Contact have no top-level heading at all. The homepage does have one, but it reads HELLO VOICE — the brand name, not a proposition. Meanwhile the portfolio page carries 52 H2s, so the heading structure conveys no hierarchy to a screen reader or a crawler.

High

No meta descriptions and no sitemap

Every page returns a null description, so Google writes its own snippet from whatever text it finds first. Titles are bare (HelloVoice, About – HelloVoice) with no service or location terms. Both standard sitemap URLs return 404, and there is no structured data on any page.

/sitemap_index.xml → 404 /wp-sitemap.xml → 404 ld+json blocks: 0
High

The contact form cannot qualify a lead

It captures name, email and message. Nothing else. No company, no service interest, no budget band, no phone number — and in this market most serious enquiries want to continue on WhatsApp. Every submission arrives needing a discovery call before you can even tell whether it is worth one.

High

A UK domain selling to Saudi Arabia, in English only

The domain is .co.uk; the only office, phone number and working hours listed are Riyadh. There is no Arabic version and no hreflang. Roughly half the decision-makers in the named client set — Nahdi, Sudair, Al-Naghi — will search and shortlist in Arabic.

Medium

No service pages and no case studies

The homepage promises "exceptional expertise in six main areas" and then never expands on any of them. There is no page to rank for "video production Riyadh" or "3D CGI Saudi Arabia", and no per-project page a salesperson can send. The portfolio is one flat page of 43 images with no filtering by service or sector.

Medium

The mobile hero is letterboxed

The 16:9 background video is not cropped for portrait, so at 375 px it sits in a narrow band with dark columns either side and the headline crowded against the logo. Mobile is likely the majority of traffic.

Medium

Details that read as neglect

The footer says Copyright © 2024. The favicon is a 150×150 crop of a file named 12345_00000-1.png, and the media library is full of similar names — 123456_00000.png, Logo_00023_00000.png, avl-ezgif.com-optimize.gif. The listed mobile number, +966 50 74488665, has one digit more than a Saudi mobile number holds and should be checked against the landline on the contact page.

Decision

Two routes, and what I would do

A · Remediate in place

Stay on WordPress and Elementor. Fix the bugs, replace the GIFs, add the missing tags and content.

  • Fastest path to a site that works
  • No migration, no retraining
  • Inherits the 27-stylesheet Elementor stack and its ceiling
  • Empty-section problem may resist a clean fix
Est. 8–12 days

B · Rebuild

New front end on a static or hybrid framework, with a proper content model for services and case studies.

  • Fixes weight and rendering structurally, not by patching
  • Real templates for services, cases, Arabic
  • Longer, and needs a content decision before design
  • Editing workflow changes for whoever updates the site
Est. 30–40 days

Do Phase 0 this week regardless of which route you pick. The broken CTA, the missing analytics and the 31.6 MB of GIFs are costing you enquiries right now, and none of those fixes is wasted if you rebuild later. Then choose: Route A if the site has to hold for another year, Route B if you want service pages, case studies and Arabic — which is the actual growth argument.

Before we scope

Six questions I need answered

Who is the site actually for?

Brand marketing managers, agency procurement, or direct founders? The IA and the tone change completely depending on the answer.

Is Arabic in scope?

This is the single biggest fork in cost and timeline. A full RTL localisation is a different project from an Arabic landing page.

Who edits the site after launch?

If an internal marketer needs to add case studies weekly, that rules some rebuild options out entirely.

Do the source clips for the five GIFs still exist?

Re-encoding from the original masters gives a far better result than transcoding the GIFs.

Which client work can be named and shown?

Pfizer, L'Oréal, Abbott and Isuzu are on the homepage as logos. Case studies need clearance, and that usually takes longer than the build.

Why .co.uk?

If the business is Riyadh-based and Gulf-facing, a .sa or .com may serve better — but a domain move has SEO consequences that need planning.
